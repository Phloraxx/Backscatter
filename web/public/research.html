Backscatter compares actual NASA–ISRO NISAR radar measurements, contextual NASA OPERA water-classification products, and fixed, reproducible signal-change methods. The central research finding is a limitation: a change in radar backscatter does not necessarily mean flooding.

Important: None of these studies provides field-validated flood extent, flood prediction, public warnings, affected-population counts, or reliable pixel-level water-change accuracy. The figures below measure agreement with satellite-derived OPERA classifications recorded on different acquisition dates.

Cyclone Arnab: a stronger, independent event test

IMD confirmed landfall on October 28, 2026, near 16.45°N, 81.9°E. We selected a 7-km-scale Godavari-delta area north of the reported landfall before inspecting its radar pixels. NISAR and OPERA provide matching before-and-after acquisitions approximately 11.2 hours apart. Actual OPERA references use Sentinel-1 tile T44QND; a neighboring tile found by the catalog was rejected once its georeferenced raster bounds proved it did not overlap the selected area.

What the water reference says

In 58,085 comparable 30 m cells, OPERA's separate Sentinel-1 classification changed from non-water to open water in 842 cells and from open water to non-water in 75 cells. These are reference-class transitions, not confirmed cyclone-caused flood area.

What the unchanged HH radar rule found

The fixed 3 dB / 3×3 median / 6-of-9 coherent radar rule captured 0 out of 842 new-open-water cells and 0 out of 75 lost-open-water cells. Its highlighted 4.4163 km² is radar-signal change, not flood extent.

The Arnab locally fitted static-water classifier scored F1 0.8987 in a spatial holdout, but its reference water-gain F1 was only 0.1347. A good single-date classification is not the same as good change detection.

NISAR scenes acquired before November 12, 2026 may contain mode-specific radiometric residuals. The affected status of these exact scenes was not established; the observed median scene change of -0.0992 dB cannot be confidently attributed to floodwater.
